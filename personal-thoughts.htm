  3. 22 JUN 2026

    On Modeling Awareness & Perceived Maturity of Decisions

    When one to several parties are involved in a certain domain of interaction where each party can sample a decision from a given state-space, each decision made, either conditionally or in a mutually exclusive manner, influences both the outcome, objectively speaking, and how that outcome is perceived by each party, subjectively speaking.


    An objective paradigm of an outcome is the least common denominator of evaluation shared by all the parties, anything above that can be considered an element of the party's subjective paradigm, which can be used to characterize that party strictly within the confines of the domain of interaction.


    The best possible outcome for each party, interacting in a domain, only takes into account its own subjective paradigm; such outcome for each party is affected by:
     • Independent occurrences of uncontrolled events
     • The non-conscious decisions made by each party involved
     • The conscious decisions that are based on a minimum amount self-awareness


    Self-awareness is the ability of each party to recognize:
     • The objective paradigm shared
     • The subjective characteristics of each party involved including oneself


    The level of maturity of each party can be self-evaluated by the probability of each decision successfully participating in the best possible subjective outcome. However, such level can be evaluated by other parties only via the objective outcome.


    Successful participation probability of each decision in the best possible outcome for an entire domain of interaction can recursively be evaluated via an intermediate outcome resulting from decisions in a narrower domain window, and so on.

    On Modeling Highly Subjective Conflicts

    In domains of interaction that are characterized by highly subjective outcomes (e.g. crude humor, fights, etc.), the subject parties can either knowingly or unknowingly be participating in them. Additionally, such domains of interaction are characterized by having each party's states of awareness to the action of participating, in either the domain of interaction itself, or that of its consequences, which already includes how subjectively acceptable a level of consequence might be, being mutually disjoint. A recursive consequential process, in that regard, dictates that the party which results in a higher average of instance ratios, where the level of anger restraint in an instance (i.e. turn) is divided by the impact of its respective consequence taken (i.e. The level of anger restraint does not necessarily have to correlate with the impact of the consequence taken during a specific turn), emerges victorious. Such criterion is a party-specific one that is computed as a harmonic mean that is weighted by the latencies taken for reciprocating consequences during turns; moreover, such latencies are affected by the levels of anger restraint being exercised.
